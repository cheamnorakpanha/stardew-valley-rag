## Welcome to the Valley!

There's so much to do, it can be hard to know where to start. The
following guide is meant to give you a simple walkthrough of the first
week or so on the farm, as well as some tips and tricks for progressing
further.

## Player Menu

Before you start, check out the player menu (open with the B button).
There are several different tabs that keep track of your progress. Use
the shoulder buttons on your controller to scroll through them.

### Menu Tabs

-   **Inventory:** Shows available inventory space, carried items,
    funds, equipped clothing.
-   **Player Stats:** Shows player levels and professions, and wallet.
-   **Friendships:** Shows your friendship/relationship status with
    villagers, and if you've talked or given gifts recently. Clicking on
    individual villagers shows past gifts and if they are liked or
    disliked.
-   **Map:** A map of Stardew Valley. Hovering over buildings shows who
    lives there and how late the building is open.
-   **Crafting:** Shows all the crafting recipes you know and the
    materials needed to craft them. More recipes are unlocked through
    leveling and building friendships.
-   **Collections:** Contains lists of all the various items you've
    collected, including crops and forage, fish caught, artifacts,
    minerals, cooking recipes, achievements, and letters and notes
    received from villagers.
-   **Settings:** Adjust UI, volume, etc.
-   **Exit to Title**

## Planting and Tending Crops

To plant crops, you'll first need to clear the planting area of any
debris - sticks, stones, weeds - with the various tools you have in your
pack. Then, use your hoe to till up a spot for your seeds. Once they're
in the ground, give them a good watering and you're all set!

## Tools

-   **Ax:** Use to chop down trees or chop up sticks. Consumes energy.
-   **Pickaxe:** Use to break up stones or undo a tilled spot. Consumes
    energy.
-   **Hoe:** Use to till the ground before planting crops, or to dig up
    artifact spots. Consumes energy.
-   **Watering Can:** Use to water your crops after planting. Crops must
    be watered every day, unless it rains. You can refill your watering
    can at any body of water by pressing Y. Consumes energy.
-   **Scythe:** Use to cut down grass and weeds. Does not consume
    energy! If you have a silo on your farm, cutting down grass has a
    chance of producing hay, which is automatically stored in the silo.

> **Artifact spots:** Artifact spots appear wherever there is tillable
> soil, both on the farm and around the valley. These spots look like
> three small wiggling "stems" sticking up from the ground.

## Cleaning Up the Farm - Stamina and Crafting

After you've got your first seeds planted, start clearing up your farm.
Don't worry about getting it all done at once, or even getting it all
done fast - just keep enough space clear for your crops and, later, for
any buildings you want Robin to build.

Farming and clearing out land does use energy, so keep an eye on your
energy bar. You'll get a pop-up warning once you get close to the
bottom, but if you use up all your energy you'll get the "sluggish"
condition. This slows your movement way down and means you won't get all
your energy back from sleeping.

## Refilling the Energy Bar

The easiest way to do this is by eating! At the start of the game you
don't have a kitchen so you can't make your own food, but nature will
provide. As you wander around the valley, keep your eyes peeled for
seasonal forage items. Many of these can be eaten as a way to get a
little extra energy.

## Crafting Materials

Once you've collected 50 pieces of wood, open your crafting menu with
the X button and check out the crafting recipes you have so far. One of
these is a chest - make one now!

You've probably noticed by now that your inventory space is pretty
limited. Find a convenient spot for the chest and then toss in anything
you don't need at the moment - including tools.

Aside from freeing up arable land, clearing your farm also provides you
with a ton of crafting materials. Everything you dig or chop or ax up
can be used to make machines and decorations for your farm, so keep some
of everything in that handy-dandy chest you made.

Another useful early-game craft is the scarecrow: if you have more than
15 crops, you'll need one of these to keep the crows from nibbling your
tender seedlings. You'll unlock this crafting recipe after you reach
level 1 of farming.

## How to Win Friends and Influence People

Once you've used up most of your energy clearing the farm, head out of
the east exit of your farm, past the bus stop and into Pelican Town
proper.

You should have another quest to meet 27 people. This will probably take
a few days, as some of the townsfolk are a bit reclusive, but you can
get a good start just by walking into town and saying hello to anyone
you meet.

## Making Friends

Getting to know the residents of Pelican Town is a great way to become
part of the community! Raising your friendship levels unlocks special
cutscenes and items, as well as cooking and crafting recipes.

## Happy Birthday!

The best way to make friends fast is to give them gifts they like or
love on their birthday. Check the calendar outside Pierre's shop, or buy
one from Robin, to see when a birthday is coming up.

Most folks like flowers, farm fresh veggies, or home cooking. You can
also check the Stardew Wiki to see everyone's personal favorites.

## Journal: Quests and Help Wanted

Another good way to make friends, and a quick buck, is by completing the
quests in your journal.

There are several different ways to get them:

-   **Story Quests:** These are added to your journal automatically and
    are usually tutorial-type quests. Completing them will often reward
    you with money and new knowledge of how to play.
-   **Help Wanted Quests:** These are posted outside Pierre's shop. Look
    for a "!" on the bulletin board. A villager will ask you to bring
    them a particular item or complete a task. Finishing these quests
    usually gets you some cash, and a higher friendship rating with the
    villager who posted it.
-   **Special Orders:** These are similar to Help Wanted quests, but a
    bit more intense. You won't unlock these until later in your first
    year of living on the farm.

## What to Buy on Day One

When you start your day, you should have 500g in your wallet as a little
bit of start-up cash.

While it may be tempting to hold on to it, you have to spend money to
make money. It's a good idea to get a little of everything before the
season ends, but for now, you can safely spend all of your 500g on more
parsnips.

With this and the 15 parsnips you began with, you will now have about 40
parsnips to water every day, unless it rains, which can take a big chunk
out of your stamina bar.

If you'd rather save your energy, you can also just plant whatever mixed
seeds you find to boost your eventual profits from your harvest.

## Nature's Bounty: Foraging and Exploring

After you've spent a little time in town, make your way back to the
farm. You can either go north over the mountain past Robin's house, or
southwest through Cindersap Forest where Marnie's Ranch is located.

The guide recommends going through the forest for now because it's a
great place to forage.

## Spring Forage

Each season, there are various items that can be found just by wandering
around and keeping an eye on the ground.

Some of these items can be eaten for energy, some can be used in cooking
or crafting, some make great gifts, and they can also be sold for a
little extra gold.

Try to save at least one of each item - you'll need them later!

## Foraging with Tools

Just like when you cleared your farm, you can use your tools all around
the valley to chop down trees, break rocks, and cut weeds.

Everything gathered outside of the farm will eventually respawn, but it
can take some time.

## Forage Spotlight: Mixed Seeds

Planting these on your farm will randomly sprout one seasonal crop,
except for trellis crops. This is a good way to get more crops for free.

## Sweet Dreams

By now, it's probably getting late - head home and get in bed before
2am!

If you don't make it, you'll just fall asleep wherever you happen to be.
Falling asleep outside will result in a small fee from whoever finds you
and takes you home, and your energy bar will not refill all the way.

However, if you gain a level in any of your skills during the day, you
can check this in your player menu, and you will wake up with full
energy regardless of when you go to sleep.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## End of Day: Shipping and Saving

At the end of every day in Stardew Valley, two things will happen:

1.  If you've put anything in the shipping bin on your farm, you'll see
    a summary of the money you've earned by category.
2.  Your progress will save.

Once you wake up the next day, you can safely close the game. Next time
you play, you will wake up at the start of whatever day you ended on.

While this can be a little annoying if you don't want to replay the
whole day, the upside is: if something goes wrong, just turn off the
game and start the day over!

## On the Passage of Time

One day in Stardew Valley takes only 14 minutes. However, if you need to
pause your game and slow down the clock, just open a menu!

Time does not pass while any menu or dialogue box is open, except the
fishing mini-game.

## Fishing for Fun and Profit

Once you've watered your crops, you can clear some more land, but save
some time and energy for learning a new skill today: fishing!

You should have received a letter from Willy, asking you to come meet
him on the beach south of town. Head that way now and watch the cutscene
to receive your first fishing rod.

## The Fishing Mini-Game

This part of the game can be hard to get the hang of at first, but
mastering fishing can be a great way to make some easy cash during your
first year.

Catching every fish unlocks a special prize later in the game.

### Basic Mechanics

Approach the water and hold down Y to cast. You'll see a distance meter
pop up when you do this: higher quality fish appear at greater distances
from land.

Release Y to drop the line.

When a fish bites, you'll get a vibration, a sound cue, and an "!" above
your head - press Y again to hook the fish.

### The Green Bar

The first time you hook a fish, a small tutorial window will pop up
instructing you on the mechanics of the mini-game: keep the green bar
behind the fish to fill the fishing meter and catch the fish.

Press Y to move the bar up, release to let it fall.

The bar has peculiar movement physics. It needs some momentum to move
up, and can "bounce" at the bottom and top of the meter.

The guide recommends tapping Y rather than holding it down to move the
bar, giving better control over movement.

In general, it's a good strategy to move the bar gradually and keep it
towards the middle if the fish is darting up and down a lot.

### Increasing Fishing Skill

With your first rod, your fishing bar is quite small.

Leveling up your fishing skill and buying better rods will make the bar
bigger and fish will bite faster.

Better rods will also allow you to use bait and bobbers, which you can
craft or buy from Willy's shop.

If you're struggling to develop your fishing skill, you can buy a
Training Rod at Willy's shop. This rod can only catch common fish, but
it's a good way to practice.

### Fishing For Treasure

Occasionally when you hook a fish, you will also see a treasure chest
icon appear.

To get it, keep the green bar behind the chest long enough to fill its
meter - but don't let the fish get away!

Treasure chests can contain useful things like ore, bait, or even rare
weapons.

## Prime Fishing Spots

If you have some food items to refill your energy, today is a good day
to spend fishing. You can also eat some of the things you catch!

The best spots for fishing vary, but as a general rule: any location
where you can cast into deeper water is good.

For now, head over to the west side of the docks where you can see a
large stone sticking out of the water. Try and cast as close to it as
you can.

Anytime you see bubbles in the water, try and cast into them - you're
guaranteed a better bite rate.

If your pockets fill up, you can sell fish directly to Willy in his shop
to free up some room.

At this point it's safe to sell almost everything, but keep one sardine
for later.

Another good spot for fishing is the Mountain Lake on the northern part
of the map near Robin's house. The fish here are on average worth a
little more, and are a good way to make money in early game.

Since your pocket space is still limited, it's a good idea to bring a
chest to store any overflow of fish, forage, or treasure.

You can place chests anywhere on the map, but beware - if your chest is
in an NPC's way, they will destroy it when they walk through it. The
little islands on the Mountain Lake should be safe, however.

## The Museum

have unearthed some of the things that belong in the museum: artifacts

If you haven't gone yet, today's a good day to visit the museum and meet

## Museum Donations

Anything that can be donated will have a description that reads:

> "Gunther can tell you more about this"

### Artifacts

Found in artifact spots, fishing treasure chests, or artifact troves.
Can also be dropped by monsters in the Mines.

### Minerals

Found in the mines, inside of geodes, or fishing treasure chests.
Occasionally show up when searching garbage cans.

### Books

Found in artifact spots, fishing treasure chests, or digging in the
mines.

Once found, you can read these books at the museum library when you see
the book icon hovering over the shelves.

Books contain tips on gameplay, info on villagers, and a few secret
messages.

The more you donate to the museum, the more rewards you will get from
Gunther.

After reaching 60 items, you receive a special reward that unlocks a new
area in town, and if you manage to find all the artifacts and minerals
you will receive the Complete Collection achievement and a rare item.

## Catfishing

If you spent enough time fishing yesterday, your fishing skill has
probably already gone up a level or two.

While it will still be tough, this may be enough to help you catch a
rare fish: the catfish.

These fish only come out in the river on rainy days in spring and fall.

If you don't manage to catch one before the end of the season, you'll
get another chance later.

This is a gift that Willy loves, but also something you'll need for an
upcoming quest.

There's also the eel - a fish that only appears on rainy spring and fall
nights in the ocean.

This fish is also needed for a quest, and is useful in several cooking
recipes later on.

## Cash for Crops: Quality and Processing

### Crop Quality

When you harvest crops they will have different quality ratings -
normal, silver, gold, and iridium - denoted by small stars at the bottom
of the icon.

The sell price for the crop increases by roughly 25% for each level of
quality.

Higher quality crops also make better gifts, increasing your friendship
level more than a normal quality gift.

### Crop Processing

Another way to increase your profits is to process your crops and animal
products.

Almost all veggies and fruits can be put into a preserves jar or a keg
and some can go in the oil maker.

In almost every case, this is the best way to maximize your profits, but
you can always check the wiki to be sure.

## Planting for the Rest of the Season

Selling your newly harvested crops directly to Pierre also guarantees
that you'll have cash on hand to buy some more seeds.

If you haven't planted at least one of each of the Spring crops, do so
now, but the bulk of your purchase should be potatoes.

They take a little longer to grow than parsnips, but they sell for more
and have a chance to produce an extra potato when harvested.

If you get all your potatoes planted and watered today, they should be
ready just before the Egg Festival, which takes place on Spring 13.

Pierre has a special seasonal booth at this event where he sells
strawberry seeds.

These are easily the most profitable crop of the spring, but you'll only
have two weeks at the end of the season to grow them!

## Farm Cleaning Does and Don'ts

### DO

-   Leave some grass to grow. Once you have a silo and a coop or barn,
    the grass you cut has a chance of becoming hay which can be used to
    feed your animals.
-   Leave grass to grow wild on your farm so you have plenty of feed.
-   Clear out weeds, sticks, and stones so the grass has plenty of room
    to grow.

### DON'T

-   Cut down all your tree stumps.
-   When you cut down a tree, if it's not in the way of your planting
    area, leave the stump rather than chopping it down.
-   The stump will continue to spawn tree seeds, which you can let
    sprout into new trees, or dig up to use in crafting recipes.

Next, check your mail: you should have a letter from Joja Corp telling
you that the rockslide north of town has been cleared.

The way to the Mines is open!

But before running off to the mountain, water your crops, prepare some
snacks for the day, then head into town.

## Restoring the Community Center

Unlocking the Community Center opens up the first path to some "endgame"
content: you can fix up the Center, or purchase a Joja membership to
turn it into a warehouse.

If you choose restoration, you'll be collecting items to donate
throughout your first year or two to unlock rewards.

If you choose Joja, you simply have to pay cash for these rewards.

Whichever route you choose, the rewards offered by completing Community
Center "bundles" are usually very helpful in progressing in the game.

Lower tier rewards include things like seed packets, farm machines, and
food, while the higher tier rewards unlock new areas or mechanics that
make gameplay easier and more expansive.

After the cutscene ends, take a look around the Community Center to make
a strange discovery.

You'll be able to do something about this tomorrow, so be patient for
now and head north to check out the newly accessible Mines.

## Explore the Mines

Go north, above the mountain lake, to find the entrance to the Mines.

When you go inside, you'll trigger a cutscene with a villager you
haven't met yet, Marlon.

He gives you a basic sword and tells you to go exploring.

## Deeper Into the Mines...

### Treasures of the Deep

The Mines provide you with many new opportunities for foraging,
adventuring, and even fishing!

As mentioned earlier, your tools can be upgraded: the Mines are where
you'll find the ore you need to do so.

Rarer ores and gems are located further down, so the deeper you go the
better your haul.

Rocks also get harder to break as you go down, so get that pickaxe
upgraded!

### The Broken Elevator

Marlon mentioned this in his cutscene, but there is an old mine elevator
in here to help you traverse the floors.

It's broken now, but will be restored with every fifth floor you reach:
5th, 10th, 15th etc.

This will allow you to resume your journey on any floor where an
elevator is located, as long as you've discovered it.

### Monster Hunter

Be warned: the Mines are also infested with monsters!

At earlier levels, they are relatively weak, but they get stronger the
deeper you go.

Monsters will usually drop resources or items when slain, so be brave
and take a swing at a few!

### The Health Bar

Stepping into the mines for the first time will also reveal your Health
bar.

Taking hits from monsters lowers your health, but like energy, this can
also be restored with food.

Dropping to zero health results in passing out and waking up in Harvey's
clinic, usually with a few items missing from your inventory.

## Now That's a Sword

The rusty sword Marlon gave you to start out with isn't great, but you
can find better weapons as you progress.

Certain levels of the Mines contain treasure chests with special
weapons, fishing treasure chests occasionally contain rare weapons, and
you can always buy weapons at Marlon's shop once you unlock it.

In a pinch, your scythe, ax, and pickaxe can also damage enemies.

## The Blocked Tunnel

You may have noticed a blocked tunnel on the right side of the first
room in the mines.

Your pickaxe won't work on that big rock, so you're going to need
something stronger.

Perhaps something...explosive?

For now, if you're feeling brave, try to reach the 5th floor of the
Mines, where the first elevator shaft is located.

Keep an eye on the clock, though: you'll need at least an hour to run
home!

## Spring 6, Year 1

## Day Six To Dew List

1.  Water crops.
2.  Find the Wizard's Tower.
3.  Donate to the Community Center.

When you check your mail today, you should have a mysterious letter from
the wizard Razmodius, asking you to seek him out in his wizardly tower.

If you haven't already located it on your map, Razmodius's tower is
south of the farm, on the west side of Cindersap Forest.

Water your crops, and harvest anything that's ready, then head down to
meet the wizard.

## The Wizard Razmodius

Your first meeting with the wizard will be an interesting cutscene where
he helps you decipher your strange discovery in the Community Center.

You can head that way for now, but befriending the wizard in the future
is a good idea.

His magical powers can help you build special buildings on your farm, or
even change your appearance!

## Donating to the Community Center

Now that you've been introduced to the Junimos and can read their
language, you can start donating their requested items to the bundles in
the Community Center.

If you've been diligent in your foraging, by now you should have one of
each of the Spring foraging items:

-   Dandelion
-   Daffodil
-   Leek
-   Wild Horseradish

If you don't, don't worry! You've got the rest of Spring to find them.

Pick them up from your farm, then head over to the Community Center to
donate them.

Your reward for this will be a packet of 30 Spring Seeds.

These are a special kind of seed that takes 7 days to grow, and will
sprout into seasonal foraging items instead of crops.

When they start to grow, they may look dead, but don't worry! They're
not.

Plant them now and use them for extra energy or cash when they sprout.

If there's still some daylight left, you can always do some foraging,
mining, or fishing.

## Spring 7, Year 1

You've made it to the last day of your first week - congratulations!

There is one important event today: Mayor Lewis's birthday!

You're unlikely to have got your hands on any of his favorite gifts yet,
but he'll still like any fresh veggies you have, or a nice daffodil.

## The Traveling Cart

When you went to visit the Wizard on Day 5, you may have noticed a
pig-drawn wagon parked in the forest.

This is the Traveling Cart, a special merchant who appears here on
Fridays and Sundays.

Her goods, smuggled in from the neighboring empire, are usually quite
pricey, but she will often carry out-of-season fish, forageables, and
veggies that might be useful if you missed something in a previous
season.

There are a few special items that rotate into her stock to keep an eye
out for.

## Rare Seeds

These are only obtainable from the Traveling Cart, and are needed for a
special quest later in the game.

They grow in Fall and take the whole season to grow, so try and grab a
couple before then if you can afford it.

## Rarecrow #4

There are 8 "rarecrows" in the game, and collecting all of them gives
you a special prize.

## Coffee Beans

Coffee Beans can be planted to grow a coffee plant, which makes more
coffee beans, and 5 beans placed in a keg will make coffee.

This gives a speed boost when consumed, which can be handy in the early
game.

The cart is the only place to buy these, but they are also dropped by
Dust Sprites in the mines, so only buy from the cart if you're just
really craving a speed boost.

## Red Cabbage Seeds

If you're hoping to finish the Community Center quickly, this is a
must-buy.

One of the bundles requires a red cabbage, but Pierre won't start
selling the seeds until Summer of Year 2.

## Play at Your Own Pace

From here, how you continue your journey is up to you.

The guide author notes that they tend to lean towards a min-maxing type
of strategy and like to always be doing something that will advance the
game.

However, Stardew Valley is designed for you to play however you want.

There is very little you can miss by not doing something fast enough.

Take your time, enjoy the vibes, and play in a way that's fun for you.

## Complete Spring Crops & Spring Foraging Bundles

These are the easiest bundles to finish in Spring, and you won't get
another chance at them till next year.

### Spring Forage

-   Daffodil

## Other Bundles

None of the other bundles absolutely need to be completed in Spring, but
here are some you can get started:

-   **Quality Crops Bundle:** 5 gold-star parsnips.
-   **River Fish Bundle:** Sunfish, catfish, shad.
-   **Lake Fish Bundle:** Largemouth bass, carp, bullhead.
-   **Ocean Fish Bundle:** Sardine.
-   **Night Fishing Bundle:** Bream, eel.
-   **Crab Pot Bundle:** Can be fully completed with the help of a crab
    pot.
-   **All Boiler Room Bundles:** If you make it to at least Level 80 in
    the mines, this room can be fully completed. It might be tricky to
    do this early in the game, but will unlock the Minecarts to help get
    you around town faster.

## The East Beach Bridge

On the east side of the beach is a broken bridge that needs 300 wood to
repair.

This opens the way for more foraging and some good fishing as well.

## Salmonberry Season

**Spring 15 - 18**

You'll see an announcement for this on Livin' Off the Land, and it's
definitely worth it to spend a few days gathering these berries.

The best spot is Cindersap Forest.

They're not worth a lot of gold, but if you keep a stack of them in your
pocket, you'll have enough food to get you through the rest of the
Spring and maybe even beyond.

## Crafting

By now you likely already have a furnace, a scarecrow, maybe even some
fences.

Here are a few other crafts you should unlock soon that are useful:

-   **Tappers:** Place these on trees to harvest oak resin, pine tar, or
    maple syrup.
-   **Crab Pot:** Useful for completing the bundle in the Community
    Center. Must be loaded with bait to catch anything.
-   **Basic Fertilizer:** If you've been cutting down trees on your farm
    and elsewhere, you probably have a lot of sap by now. That's all it
    takes to craft this simple fertilizer. Placing it on tilled ground
    before seeds have sprouted gives them a slightly higher chance of
    being high quality, which means more profit!
-   **Sprinklers:** Getting tired of watering your crops every day? Then
    these are a must have. The regular sprinklers are the quickest to
    make, but it's a good idea to upgrade to quality sprinklers once you
    unlock them.

## Save Up for Summer Planting

Don't forget to save up money for summer seeds!

On the first day of summer, all your spring crops will die off and
you'll need to get your garden ready for summer seeds.

For summer, you'll once again want a little of everything, but
blueberries are the crop that gives you the best profit.

And if you can manage it, buy an apple tree and plant it on the first
day of summer.

It takes all season to grow, and apples are some of the hardest fruits
to get a hold of.

## Tool Upgrades

Once you start getting a good amount of copper, it's time to start
upgrading those tools.

The order you do this in is up to you, but keep the following in mind:

-   It takes Clint 2 days to upgrade your tools.
-   You'll need to prioritize pickaxe if you want to go deeper in the
    mines.
-   Try and have your hoe and watering can upgraded before the end of
    season to speed up your summer planting.
-   The best time to upgrade the watering can is the day before a rainy
    day, or on the 27th of the month.

## The Flower Dance

**Spring 24**

During this event, you have the chance to ask one of the local singles
to be your dance partner.

However, they'll all turn you down unless you manage to get your
friendship to 4 hearts.

This is tricky, but not impossible if you are diligent in chatting them
up and gift giving.

If you've got your eye on someone early in the season, check their wiki
page to see what kinds of gifts they like for a chance at Spring
romance.
